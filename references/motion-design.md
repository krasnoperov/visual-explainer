# Motion as explanation

## Preserve the source model

Treat motion as a time-based rendering of the original explanation, not an animation of the HTML artifact.

Keep these inputs available throughout production:

- the user's question and intended decision;
- original sources and evidence;
- verified claims, inference, proposals, and caveats;
- the semantic brief and relationship map;
- the static artifact, when one exists, as a visual reference rather than the sole content source.

For every beat, record:

| Field | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Viewer learns | What becomes newly understandable? |
| Relationship | Sequence, cause, convergence, hierarchy, transformation, comparison, scale, or state? |
| Evidence | Which real payload, quote, event, value, screenshot, or code fragment supports it? |
| Visible action | What changes on screen to expose that relationship? |
| Context carried | What from the preceding beat must remain visible or mentally active? |

Delete a beat that cannot answer all five fields. Do not repeat the same claim merely to create rhythm.

## Compose for time

- Rewrite at the meaning layer. HTML groups information for inspection; video orders changes in understanding. Their section boundaries do not need to match.
- Reveal a useful model or piece of evidence immediately. Do not spend the opening on a title card or animated setup.
- Keep continuity on screen when it matters. Let earlier evidence accumulate, recede, connect, or transform instead of clearing the canvas for every beat.
- Give simple relationships less time and difficult boundaries more time. Do not assign equal durations merely because sections are siblings.
- Use one dominant action per beat. Cuts and transitions must express a change of subject, scale, state, or causal position.
- Compose for the likely phone-sized viewing surface. Recompose deliberately for other aspect ratios; do not center-crop essential information.

## Use semantic motion

Prefer actions whose geometry still communicates when labels are hidden:

- advance a marker through a timeline while consequences accumulate;
- converge independent evidence into one supported conclusion;
- propagate one cause through a fan-out of effects;
- morph aligned before/after states to expose the exact difference;
- move an item across a boundary to show a state or trust transition;
- update a ledger row from unknown to verified when evidence arrives;
- zoom from a system overview into one boundary while retaining orientation;
- change scale or proportion against an honest denominator.

Reject these defaults:

- heading, paragraph, and bullets entering one after another;
- a grid of labelled cards used as a substitute for six diagrams;
- the static report scrolled or revealed section by section;
- arbitrary fades, springs, zooms, or transitions that do not alter meaning;
- voiceover and on-screen copy stating the same sentence;
- long title or premise screens that delay the first evidence.

If the primary description of a shot is “text appears,” redesign or delete it.

## Divide work between picture, text, and sound

- Let picture demonstrate relationships and state changes.
- Use on-screen text for short labels, exact values, quotes, identifiers, and conclusions that must survive silent playback.
- Use narration, when appropriate, for causal connective tissue, interpretation, and caveats. Write it to be spoken, not read from a report.
- If narration is absent, simplify the story rather than moving the entire explanation onto the screen.
- Keep captions readable and synchronized without covering the visual evidence.

## Temporal gates

- Show useful information immediately.
- Do not leave long intervals without a change in understanding; more movement without new meaning does not count.
- Hold only when inspection is the intended action and the evidence earns the reading time.
- Watch the animatic and final encode from beginning to end at 1× on a likely phone-sized display.
- Record timecodes for the first wait, first ambiguity, first repeated composition, and first impulse to skip. Fix the story or timing before polish.
- Review the opening, all transitions, silent playback, and audio-only playback when narration exists.
- Use contact sheets to catch composition and continuity problems, never as a substitute for full playback.

The final question is not “does everything appear?” It is “what became easier to understand because it moved?”
